EARNINGS / GUIDANCE RAISEEvonik26 Jun 2026
Evonik raises full-year 2026 outlook after a strong Q2 — but flags the tailwind will fade
Evonik published preliminary Q2 2026 figures with adjusted EBITDA of €600–650 million, well above the ~€567 million Vara Research consensus, and raised full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance to €2.0–2.2 billion from €1.7–2.0 billion. Management attributed the upside to supply bottlenecks hitting Asian competitors after the Strait of Hormuz blockade and higher Asian energy costs, while warning of uncertainty in H2 if global supply chains stabilise.
STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS
→The raise is the clearest evidence that the Middle-East shock is transferring demand and pricing power to European specialty producers.
→It is crisis-driven, not structural — H2 guidance language across the sector is the key thing to watch.
SOURCEEuropean Coatings / Evonik — 26 Jun 2026
ANALYST / SENTIMENTSectorJun 2026
JPMorgan downgrades European chemicals as the Middle-East boost fades; Goldman turns pro-cyclical
JPMorgan cut Evonik, Arkema and distributor IMCD to Underweight, arguing the Q2/Q3 supply-chain tailwind from the Middle-East conflict is short-lived and that 2027 estimates will fall sharply once it rolls off. It raised BASF's 2026 numbers but cut 2027 in equal measure, and kept Overweight ratings on more differentiated names — Croda, Novonesis, Fuchs, Solvay, Syensqo and Umicore. Goldman Sachs separately shifted to a more pro-cyclical stance, and S&P Global noted a Strait of Hormuz reopening could offset the fading tailwind.
STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS
→The Street is split: cyclical optimism (Goldman) vs. "enjoy it while it lasts" (JPMorgan).
→Differentiated specialty/ingredient portfolios are being rewarded over commodity-exposed names.
SOURCEInvesting.com / S&P Global — Jun 2026

NUTRITION / SUPPLY CHAINEvonik26 Jun 2026
Evonik secures European methionine supply with a new methanol pipeline
A new dedicated methanol (MMP precursor) pipeline — developed with Lülsdorf Functional Solutions, Röhm and SYNEQT Pipelines — became operational in June. Methanol imported via Lülsdorf is piped to Evonik's Wesseling site and the intermediate is then supplied to Antwerp, where it is converted into MetAMINO® (DL-methionine) for livestock nutrition.

PERSONAL CARE / REGULATORYBASF9 Jun 2026
FDA approves BASF's bemotrizinol (Tinosorb® S) — first new US sunscreen filter in 25 years
The US FDA approved bemotrizinol (Bis-Ethylhexyloxyphenol Methoxyphenyl Triazine), marketed by BASF as Tinosorb® S, as a permitted OTC sunscreen active — the first new sunscreen ingredient cleared in the US since 1999. The broad-spectrum, photostable UVB/UVA filter has a long use history in Europe; the final order takes effect 9 August 2026, with first US sunscreens expected in late 2026–2027.
STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS
→A landmark win for European UV-filter chemistry and a new US growth runway for BASF Care Chemicals.
→Reinforces "proven efficacy + photostability + broad spectrum" as the personal-care innovation axis.
SOURCEBASF Press Release — 9 Jun 2026
ENERGY / GEOPOLITICSHormuz17 Jun 2026
Hormuz ceasefire MOU signed then breached — European energy markets stay volatile
Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz has been largely blocked since 28 February 2026. On 17 June, the US and Iran signed a memorandum of understanding to end the war and the blockade, but Iran re-closed the strait within days citing alleged violations. EXECUTIVE BRIEF — WEEK 22–28 JUN 2026
European Chemicals: Hormuz Tailwind Lifts Q2 Earnings — Analysts Warn It Fades

Overall Assessment: The week's dominant signal is that the Middle-East supply shock is now visibly boosting European chemical earnings. Evonik raised full-year guidance on a strong Q2, explicitly crediting supply bottlenecks at Asian competitors after the Strait of Hormuz blockade. Yet the same week brought a clear counter-signal: JPMorgan downgraded Evonik, Arkema and IMCD because that tailwind is temporary, while Goldman turned pro-cyclical and S&P flagged that a Hormuz reopening could erase the benefit. Underneath the cyclical noise, Cefic's data (Q1 output −3.2%, utilisation ~74%, 37 Mt of capacity closing since 2022) confirms the structural crisis is unchanged.

1. The Tailwind Is Real — And Showing Up in Guidance
Evonik's Q2 adjusted EBITDA of €600–650M beat consensus by ~15% and drove a full-year raise to €2.0–2.2B. The driver is reduced Asian import competition and pricing power, not a genuine demand recovery in Europe.
2. …But the Market Is Already Pricing the Fade
JPMorgan cut three names to Underweight and trimmed 2027 estimates even where it raised 2026. The split with Goldman's pro-cyclical pivot shows how much hinges on the durability of the Hormuz disruption — a binary, geopolitics-driven outcome.
3. Structural Backdrop Unchanged
EU gas remains ~3.3× US levels, capacity utilisation sits near 74% (heavy chemicals 62–68%), and Cefic counts 37 Mt of capacity closed or closing since 2022 against new investment adding only ~2%. Surcharges of up to 30% underline the energy-cost squeeze.
4. Portfolio Reshaping & Selective Innovation Continue
BASF Coatings goes standalone on 1 July; LyondellBasell's "Velogy" divestment is complete; and capital still flows to differentiated bets — Avantium's PEF flagship, Evonik's methionine logistics, and BASF's Tinosorb® S US approval all landed this month.

Calls require robust techno-economic and life-cycle evidence vs. conventional routes.
TREND SIGNAL
Public funding is steering R&D toward CO₂ utilisation, chemical recycling and waste-to-feedstock — aligning industrial R&D with the Clean Industrial Deal.

MARKET OUTLOOK — JUNE 2026
Cyclical — A Real but Temporary Tailwind
The Strait of Hormuz blockade is constraining Asian competitors (higher energy, logistics bottlenecks), handing European producers temporary pricing power and share gains. Evonik's guidance raise is the clearest evidence; BASF, Lanxess and others have flagged similar dynamics. The key risk is reversal: a durable Hormuz reopening would erase the benefit quickly (S&P, JPMorgan).
Structural — The Crisis Is Unchanged
Beneath the cycle, the picture is unchanged: Q1 output −3.2%, utilisation ~74% (heavy chem 62–68%), EU gas ~3.3× US, and 37 Mt of capacity closed or closing since 2022 against new investment of only ~2%. Energy cost remains the binding competitiveness constraint, with surcharges up to 30%.
Energy — Volatile and Policy-Sensitive
Gas and oil remain war-elevated and headline-driven after the 17 June ceasefire MOU was breached. EU electricity-market and energy-cost relief measures (electricity-tax reform, gas-storage coordination) remain critical to the sector's structural cost position.
Feedstocks & Structure
US ethane-based crackers (INEOS Project ONE) will hold a durable cost advantage over European naphtha crackers on commissioning. Restructuring continues through capacity exits, carve-outs (BASF Coatings, LYB/Velogy) and selective investment in recycling, bio-based polymers and specialty/biotech actives.
